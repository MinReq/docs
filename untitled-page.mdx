phone.systems™ is a cloud-based PBX for building and managing business call routing. It lets you receive calls, route them through visual call flows, connect users and destinations, manage audio and delivery options, and review call activity from a web-based interface.

Use this overview when you need to understand the main parts of phone.systems™ before configuring users, numbers, contact methods, call flows, or the phone.systems™ app.

## What phone.systems™ is

phone.systems™ provides a hosted PBX environment where call handling is configured in the cloud. Instead of managing a physical PBX, you configure users, numbers, contact methods, trunks, schedules, and call flows in the phone.systems™ interface.

The platform can be used to build simple or complex call routing logic, including direct user routing, ring groups, voice menus, queues, voicemail, call recording, fax delivery, notifications, and forwarding.

## How phone.systems™ works

phone.systems™ uses several connected parts:

- **Call Flows** define how calls move through the system.
- **Numbers** receive external calls or provide internal extension dialing.
- **Users** represent people who can use app devices and contact methods.
- **Contact Methods** define destinations such as app lines, SIP accounts, PSTN forwarding, SIP forwarding, and email delivery.
- **Time Schedules** apply time-based routing logic.
- **Audio Files** provide announcements, prompts, and music-on-hold.
- **Delivery Methods** define where voicemail, fax, notification, and recording files are sent.
- **Trunks** connect phone.systems™ to inbound or outbound SIP routing.
- **Call Analytics** provides call records and statistics.
- **Settings** control system-wide configuration, integrations, feature codes, storage, AI settings, and technical options.

## Call flows

Call flows are the main routing model in phone.systems™. A call flow is built by adding objects to a visual workspace and connecting them with cables.

Objects define what happens to a call. For example, a call may enter through a Phone Number object, pass through a Voice Menu object, route to a Ring Group, and then continue to Voicemail if the call is not answered.

## Users and destinations

Users can be linked to app devices and contact methods. Contact methods define where calls or files are delivered. For example, a user may have a phone.systems™ app line, a SIP account, a PSTN forwarding number, or an email contact method.

These destinations can then be used in call flows, ring groups, voicemail delivery, fax delivery, notifications, and other features.

## Numbers and trunks

Numbers define how calls enter or move inside phone.systems™. Phone numbers can receive external calls, while internal numbers can be used for extension dialing.

Trunks define SIP connectivity. Inbound trunks receive SIP traffic from external providers into phone.systems™. Termination gateways and termination routes define how outbound SIP traffic is sent to external destinations.

## Media, delivery, and reporting

Audio files and playlists provide prompts, announcements, and music-on-hold. Delivery methods define how generated files, such as voicemails, faxes, notifications, and call recordings, are sent to destinations.

Call Analytics provides call flow CDRs, termination CDRs, and statistics that help monitor activity and troubleshoot call behavior.

## When to use phone.systems™

Use phone.systems™ when you need to:

- build cloud-based call routing without managing a physical PBX
- route calls between users, teams, departments, or destinations
- configure voice menus, queues, voicemail, fax, call recording, and notifications
- use app devices or SIP-compatible devices for calling
- manage inbound and outbound SIP routing
- review call records and performance statistics
